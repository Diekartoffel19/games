📖 Wall Street Trader Pro — Complete Guide

📊 1. Financial Dashboard Metrics Explained
Net Worth: The true total value of your financial empire. Calculated as: Available Cash + Current Market Value of Holdings - Bank Debt.
Available Cash & Buying Power: The liquid funds currently uninvested, ready to buy stocks.
Portfolio Value: The combined dollar value of your active long and short positions if liquidated at current market bids.
Day Total P&L: How much money your portfolio made or lost today (both closed profits and unrealized swings).
Trader Level: Your Wall Street prestige rank (from Intern to Wolf of Wall Street), unlocking higher status as your wealth compounds.
🛒 2. How to Trade: Buttons & Order Types Explained
BUY / LONG: Purchase shares expecting the price to increase. When you sell later at a higher price, you keep the difference as profit.
SELL / SHORT: Sell shares you own OR initiate a "Short Position". Short selling allows you to borrow shares to sell now and buy back later at a cheaper price, making a profit when stocks crash!
Market Order: Executes instantly at the current best available price in the order book. Great for fast entries.
Limit Order: Sets a target price. Your order will only trigger and fill if the market reaches or beats your specified price.
Stop-Loss Order: A crucial risk-management order that automatically liquidates your shares if the price drops to your stop price, protecting you from huge losses.
25%, 50%, 75%, MAX Buttons: Instantly calculates how many shares you can buy with that percentage of your available cash.
📈 3. Chart Indicators & Technical Tools Explained
Candlesticks (OHLC): Each bar shows the Open, High, Low, and Close price for that period. Green candles closed higher than they opened; Red candles closed lower. The thin wicks show the extreme highs and lows.
SMA(20) — Simple Moving Average: The yellow line plots the average closing price over the last 20 periods. If the stock price is above the SMA line, it indicates an upward trend.
B-Bands — Bollinger Bands: Purple volatility bands set 2 standard deviations above and below the average price. When prices hit the upper band, the stock may be overextended; when they touch the lower band, it may be oversold.
RSI(14) — Relative Strength Index: The momentum graph at the bottom ranging from 0 to 100. Values above 70 mean the stock is Overbought (buyers exhausted, pullback likely). Values below 30 mean it is Oversold (panic selling, potential bounce).
⚡ 4. Realistic Mechanics: Order Book & Slippage
Bid vs Ask & Spread: The Bid is the highest price waiting buyers will pay; the Ask is the lowest price sellers will accept. The difference is the Spread.
Level 2 Order Book: The real-time queue of waiting buy and sell orders. Clicking any row in the order book automatically loads that price into your trade terminal!
Market Impact & Slippage: If you place a huge order on an illiquid stock like MEME or PENY, your order will consume available shares in the book and physically drive the price up or down!
Bank Margin Loan: Allows you to borrow up to 50% of your net worth from the bank to leverage trades. Incurs a 0.1% daily interest fee.
Dividends: Blue-chip companies like AAPL and TITAN distribute cash dividends directly into your balance at the start of each new trading day!
